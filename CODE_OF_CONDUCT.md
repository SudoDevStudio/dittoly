# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to the people you work with here. Discuss the code, not the person who wrote it. Assume the other person
is trying to make the project better, even when you think they are wrong.

## What is expected

- Keep criticism about the work: what the change does, what it breaks, what it would take to accept it.
- Say what you mean plainly, and leave people a way to answer.
- Accept that maintainers decide what goes in, and that a "no" can come with a reason you disagree with.
- Respect the privacy of anyone who reports a security problem, and of anyone whose data appears in a bug report.

## What is not

- Personal attacks, insults, or dismissing someone for who they are rather than what they wrote.
- Harassment of any kind, in the open or in private messages that started here.
- Publishing someone's private information, or anything from a security report before it is public.
- Deliberately wasting people's time: repeated off-topic posts, or arguing a settled decision again and again.

## Where this applies

Everywhere this project happens: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and any space that represents
the project.

## If something goes wrong

Report it privately through GitHub on this repository — **Security → Report a vulnerability** reaches the
maintainers privately and may be used for this too, if you have no other private channel. Say what happened and
where; include links if there are any.

Maintainers will look at it, keep the report to themselves as far as they can, and answer. Depending on what
happened, the answer may be a word in private, an edit, or removing someone from the project's spaces. A maintainer
who is the subject of a report takes no part in deciding it.

This is adapted in spirit from the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org), kept short on
purpose: a short page people read beats a long one nobody does.
